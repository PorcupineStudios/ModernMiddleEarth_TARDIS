# Borders and kingdoms

Read when the task touches `border/`, kingdoms, the map's border layer, or spawning in land that changed hands.
Built 2026-11-18 (Aaron: kingdoms of several colonies; found your own kingdom with its own alignment, shown on the
map, or pledge to an existing faction; borders grow through war, like LOTR 1.7.10's conquest; the player map changes,
terrain generation never does; keep the original map and forward the changes onto it; hooks for spawning).

## Layers

Who holds a map pixel (128 x 128 blocks) is read top down:

1. **Marks** (`Borders.mark`): land set by an operator (`/lotr border mark <radius> <faction|nobody|clear>`) or, later,
   a war goal. Kept until cleared.
2. **Claims**: what colonies hold. Each colony in Middle-earth reaches `claim_radius` pixels from its Hall, plus one per
   whole `people_per_pixel` people, up to `max_radius`; at a pixel its strength is radius minus distance, the strongest
   takes it, ties go to the older colony (`TerritoryMath`, JUnit `TerritoryMathTest`). Worked out again on every
   founding, transfer and disbanding (`SocietyEvent.ColonyFounded/ColonyTransferred/ColonyDisbanded`) and every
   `recompute_ticks`. A colony's holder is its faction: a kingdom (realm) or a great power.
3. **The original map** (`BorderData.base`, `data/<ns>/border_base/*.json`: biome -> faction), generated by
   `converter/borders.py` from the NPC spawn tables: the faction whose people spawn most in a biome holds it; biomes
   whose leading people only come out at night (orcs roaming Eriador) and seas are nobody's. Play never writes it.

Both saved layers (`Borders`, SavedData `lotr_port_borders`, `dataVersion` 1, layers grouped by holder) hold **only
pixels that differ from the layer below**: the original map stays as it was and the changes are forwarded onto it.
Clearing a change (a colony disbanded, a mark cleared) gives the original holder back. Every change is a history line
`border_changed` (from, to, pixels, one sample pixel, reason).

**World generation never reads borders.** A conquered Shire still generates Shire biomes, structures and terrain
(GameTest checks the biome). What borders change: the player map, spawning, conditions.

## Kingdoms

- A kingdom is a realm (`faction/Realms`): its own faction with alignment, colour and relations (its culture's until
  changed in Diplomacy). `/lotr_kingdom found <name> [#rrggbb]` (`Kingdoms.found`): renounces a pledge (that people
  becomes the culture; without one, the player's race's first people), starts the ruler at `ruler_alignment`, and
  moves the ruler's colony into the kingdom as its capital. `kingdom_needs_colony` (default false) can require one.
- A player either rules a kingdom or pledges. Colonies are founded for the ruler's kingdom, else for the pledge
  (`Kingdoms.colonyFaction`, used by the Colony Charter). Other players pledge to a player kingdom with
  `kingdom_pledge_alignment` (realm factions get one rank, `subject`); their colonies are the kingdom's too. A ruler
  can't pledge to their own kingdom; a ruler pledging to a great power makes the kingdom its vassal (as before).
- A kingdom grows by founding colonies and by taking them in war (a won `take_colony` war is `Colonies.transfer`,
  which moves the land with the colony).
- `/lotr_kingdom info` (colonies, squares beyond old land), `/lotr_kingdom land` (who holds here, and who did).
  Admin: `/lotr border recompute|gains|mark`.

## Player map

`BorderNet.Snapshot` (on login and after every change): the holders (id, name, colour), the original holder per biome
index (the client has the biome map), the changed pixels and their holders, and kingdom names at their capitals
(each realm's oldest colony). `client/BorderLayer` makes one texture at half the map's resolution (a changed pixel wins
its 2 x 2 texel): a light wash of each holder's colour, stronger along borders. `MapScreen` draws it over the map,
names kingdoms at their capitals, and the hover lines say "Land of X (once Y)". Key B toggles it; client config
`map.borders` is the first value.

## Spawning hooks

`NpcSpawner` asks `TerritorySpawns.adjust` for every people table (not LOTR's animal tables) at the spot it picked.
Land with its original holder keeps the biome's table. In taken land, with `conquered_share` the holder's people come
(`data/<ns>/border_spawns/<faction ns>/<faction path>.json`, npc_spawns format, generated by `borders.py` from the
biomes each faction holds; a kingdom sends its culture's people), else the biome's people with the holder's enemies
weighted by `enemy_factor`. Then `TerritorySpawns.SpawnEvent` (holder, original holder, taken, biome table, table;
`setTable`) is posted on the NeoForge bus for every people spawn in Middle-earth, so KubeJS, other mods and later
systems (a kingdom's own races, garrisons, invasions) can change or clear it.

Condition `lotr_port:territory` (`holder`, `lotr_port:nobody`, `taken`) for quests, unlocks, story and research.

## Settings

`data/lotr_port/borders/settings.json`: `claim_radius`, `people_per_pixel`, `max_radius`, `recompute_ticks`,
`conquered_share`, `enemy_factor`, `kingdom_pledge_alignment`, `ruler_alignment`, `kingdom_needs_colony`. Reloads.

## Tests

JUnit `TerritoryMathTest`; GameTests `BorderTests` (batch `borders`): a realm's colony in the Shire holds the land
while the original map and the terrain's biome stay the hobbits'; a transfer moves the land; taken land spawns the
holder's people; a mark wins; disbanding gives the land back and spawns return to the biome's. A kingdom is a
pledgeable faction, its ruler's and subjects' colonies are its own.
